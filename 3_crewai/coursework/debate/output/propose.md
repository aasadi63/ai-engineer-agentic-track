I strongly support the motion that there should be stricter laws to regulate LLMs.

LLMs are powerful tools, but power without accountability is dangerous. These systems can spread misinformation at scale, manipulate public opinion, produce harmful instructions, and invade privacy by processing massive amounts of data. Left unregulated, the risks are not theoretical—they are immediate and growing. A model that can generate convincing lies, biased content, or dangerous advice for millions of people should not operate with fewer safeguards than many ordinary industries.

Regulation is not about stopping innovation. It is about making innovation safe, trustworthy, and responsible. We already regulate medicines, cars, food, and banks because society recognizes that useful technologies can also cause serious harm. LLMs deserve the same standard. Laws can require transparency about training data, safety testing, user protection, reporting of harms, and clear accountability when companies release dangerous systems.

Without restrictions, the costs are borne by the public while the profits go to a few companies. That is unjust and unsustainable. Stronger laws will protect users, preserve trust, and ensure that AI serves society rather than endangering it.

In short: if LLMs are going to shape our future, they must be governed by rules that put human safety, rights, and accountability first.
